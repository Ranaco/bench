# Parts in 3D: the rules

Every part in Bench will be in 3D, and later they'll go on a 3D breadboard. These rules make every model fit
together: the same units, the same way up, the same kind of materials, the same way to handle them. Each rule says
what backs it: **[standard]** a published standard or convention, **[datasheet]**, **[vendor]** a library or
engine's documented default, or **[ours]** a choice made here, and why.

## 1. Sizes come from the datasheet, and say where from

- Every dimension lives in a table for its series, one row per part, with the datasheet it came from. One builder
  function makes every size in the series from its row. [standard: KiCad's model generators work this way, a
  `cq_parameters.yaml` per series with a `source:` datasheet URL per entry]
- Every number is tagged with where it came from:
  - `printed`: printed on the datasheet's drawing or table
  - `scaled`: measured off the drawing's proportions, where it prints no number
  - `drawn`: not on the datasheet at all, an illustration (the inside of most parts)
  The 3D view tells you which, piece by piece. [ours: Bench never passes off a guess as a measurement]
- Where a datasheet gives a minimum ("15 min."), the model uses the minimum and says so.
- Layers too thin to see at true scale (a capacitor's oxide is about 1.3 to 1.5 nm per volt; its foils 0.02 to
  0.11 mm) are drawn thicker, and labelled as exaggerated. [datasheet: Nichicon Technical Notes CAT.8101H]
- The outside is checked against the maker's own model or KiCad's for the same part number where one exists.
  Their models are references, not parts of Bench: they're outsides only, one solid lump, and licensed
  (KiCad's 3D library is CC-BY-SA 4.0, some folders GPL-3+; makers' files usually forbid redistribution).

## 2. Units, origin and axes

- Geometry is built in **millimetres**. [standard: KiCad Library Conventions G1.9, "Linear dimensions should be
  specified in millimeters"]
- The scene is in **metres**: every model's root is scaled by 0.001. Lights in physical units, glTF files and
  physics engines all assume metres. [vendor: glTF 2.0 "The units for all linear distances are meters"; three.js
  r155 physical lighting needs "1 world unit = 1 meter"; Rapier: "Use SI units"]
- **y is up** (three.js's own convention); **y = 0 is the surface the part sits on**, the board's top. Leads go
  down into negative y. [standard: KiCad's generators put z = 0 on the board's top surface; three.js is y-up,
  so their z is our y]
- **The origin is pin 1**, on that surface. [standard: KiCad F7.2, "For through hole components the footprint
  anchor should be placed at the location of pin 1"]
- **Pin 1 → pin 2 runs along +x**: pin 1 on the left, looking at the part from the front (+z). [standard: KiCad
  F4.2, "pin 1 on the left side" for two-terminal and inline parts]
- Each model declares its pins as data, not just shapes: where each one enters the board (mm, in the model's
  frame), which way it points, and how thick it is. That's what a breadboard will snap to. [ours]

## 3. One group per piece

- A model is one group per physical piece (shaft, can, sleeve, lead…), each named, so any piece can be picked,
  highlighted, hidden or lifted away. [ours: KiCad fuses its pieces into one solid on export; Bench keeps them
  apart, because taking parts apart is how the lessons teach]
- Each piece has a card: its name, what it does in plain words, and where its shape comes from.
- Taking a part apart lifts its pieces along y, inside out, never through each other.
- A model's builder is plain code that makes three.js objects, with no React and no WebGL, so tests can check its
  sizes against the datasheet table.

## 4. Materials

- Physically based (metalness and roughness). Metals are metalness 1, everything else 0. [vendor: Filament,
  "Metallic … Should be 0 or 1"]
- Metals use measured colours from the physicallybased.info database (CC0), which are **linear** RGB values: set
  with `Color.setRGB`, never as hex. Aluminium 0.916, 0.923, 0.924; copper 0.932, 0.623, 0.522; silver 0.991,
  0.985, 0.974; stainless steel 0.669, 0.639, 0.598; brass 0.910, 0.778, 0.423. [vendor: physicallybased.info
  v2, updated 2026-11-03; matches Adobe's metal references]
- Non-metals stay inside 30–240 in sRGB (nothing blacker than soot, nothing whiter than snow). [vendor: Filament
  material guide; Adobe PBR guide]
- No measured values exist for tin (the plating on most leads), the plastics, rubber or circuit boards. Those use
  the nearest database stand-in (rubber: "Tire", 0.023; carbon: "Charcoal", 0.02) or KiCad's library colours for
  bodies (black body #262525, light label print #b0a998, white body #e4e3cf), and are marked as stand-ins.
- Roughness has no measured values anywhere; every roughness is a stand-in.
- Printed markings (a capacitor's value, its − stripe) are textures in sRGB. [vendor: three.js colour
  management, "texture.colorSpace = SRGBColorSpace" for colour textures]

## 5. Rendering

- Colour managed: sRGB output, linear maths. [vendor: three.js r152+ defaults]
- **Neutral tone mapping**, which keeps base colours as they are under white light, not ACES, which shifts them.
  [vendor: Khronos PBR Neutral; Google model-viewer's default since v4, whose docs say ACES "has serious
  color-accuracy problems"]
- Lit by a studio environment made in code (soft area lights), so nothing loads from the internet.
- A soft contact shadow under the part, not shadow maps: one cheap pass instead of re-drawing the scene per light.
  [vendor: three.js shadows manual; `webgl_shadow_contact` example] It depends only on the part's shape, so it's
  drawn again only when the shape changes (taken apart, unrolled), not every frame. [ours]
- Antialiased. Near and far planes fitted to the part: depth precision is spread between them. [vendor: three.js
  cameras manual]
- Drawn only when something changes, never in a loop. While the view moves, it drops its resolution if frames are
  slow (a phone, a weak graphics chip), and sharpens again when it stops. Everything it made is freed when the view
  closes.

## 6. Handling

- **Targets**: anything you tap is at least 44 × 44 px on a touch screen. [standard: Apple HIG 44 pt; Android
  48 dp; WCAG 2.5.8 AAA 44 px]
- **Every drag has a tap or key alternative**: turn a knob with the arrow keys or the slider; later, place a part
  by tapping it, then tapping a hole. [standard: WCAG 2.5.7 (AA), "single pointer without dragging"]
- Dragging a part's handle (a knob's shaft) moves the part, not the camera; the camera stops while you do. [vendor:
  three.js `misc_controls_transform` example, `orbit.enabled = !event.value`]
- There's always a way back to the starting view. [vendor: model-viewer: without one, getting back is "effectively
  impossible"]
- One gesture is one undo step. [standard: Apple HIG undo: batch "incremental adjustments to a single property"]
- Motion respects "reduce motion": things that would glide jump instead. [standard: prefers-reduced-motion]

## 7. Physics

- **Now: real limits, not a physics engine.** Parts move the way their datasheets allow and no further: a knob
  turns 280° between its stops, a slide switch slides between its two sides, a button travels down and springs
  back.
- **Leads snap into holes.** A lead near a hole shows where it will go, and drops in when you let go. That's how
  "push this into that" is done in practice, and it tells the circuit which hole the lead is in. [vendor: Unity XR
  socket interactor: snap to an attach point, with a hover preview]
- **Later, with the 3D breadboard: Rapier** for parts that fall, rest and get pushed around. It's deterministic,
  has joints with limits (revolute for a knob, prismatic for a switch) and continuous collision detection, and
  is about 1.7 MB compressed, loaded only with the 3D bench. [vendor: Rapier docs] Its sizes need care: physics
  engines are built for objects 0.1 to 10 m, and a 0.6 mm lead is thinner than their default contact margins
  (Jolt's is 20 mm). [vendor: Jolt docs; Bullet manual "Minimum object size … about 0.2 units"]. Prove it with a
  test before relying on it.

## 8. The stack

- three.js (pinned), drawn through react-three-fiber, with drei for the camera controls, labels, environment and
  contact shadow. [vendor: fiber 9 is the React 19 line; drei 10 and @react-three/rapier 2 pair with it]
- All of it loads only when the 3D view first opens.
